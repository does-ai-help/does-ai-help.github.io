# Citation-Following Update: What Recent Work Adds

Search and inspection: **1 November 2026**. Eleven admitted sources bring November's intake to **98 records**, or **327** combined with October. This is a targeted citation-following search, not an exhaustive review or a count of independent studies. [Search log and inspected citation edges](../november/search-2026-11-01.json).

## Main Findings

### Task Expansion Has a Stronger Empirical Test

[Brosch et al., IZA DP 18999](https://docs.iza.org/dp18999.pdf) supply randomized evidence relevant to the possibility of newly feasible work, beyond practitioner accounts. The study compares tasks within and outside apprentices' curriculum. [@broschtasks2026]

Its immediate comprehension measures should not be relabeled durable learning. Participants were instructed not to use aids during comprehension, but internet access remained available; the authors report monitoring and an exclusion sensitivity check. Open answers were graded by a blinded LLM using teacher-validated rubrics, with alternative-grader checks. These are relevant safeguards, not independent human replication. Compare its intervention, population and outcome with Shen/Tamkin before treating the results as contradictory. [@broschtasks2026; @shen2026]

**Synthesis implication:** the favorable task-expansion hypothesis now has a more direct experiment in our collection. Preserve both that contribution and its boundary: successful short tasks and immediate comprehension do not establish long-run expertise or production delivery.

### Recent Reviews Improve the Question More Than the Effect Estimate

[Fan et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00548-y), published 30 October, offer a conceptual framework separating learning and performance. The authors explicitly state that the proposed model is not empirically validated. Their discussion permits instrumental delegation where learning is not the objective; it also warns against excessive scaffolding. [@fanmetacognition2026]

[Riva et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38647), submitted 28 October, connect individual and organizational cognitive capital with joint human-AI capability. This is a narrative synthesis with overlapping studies, not a new longitudinal dataset. Its organizational mechanisms are partly inferred from individual evidence. [@rivacapital2026]

[Inie et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2026.2722522), published in September, examine construct validity across HCI research. Their scoping map does not quality-weight the reported directions of influence. This is particularly useful when apparent disagreement results from different definitions and measures. Direct publisher access failed; indexed primary sections and authoritative metadata, not complete full text, were inspected. [@iniecritical2026]

**Synthesis implication:** do not turn the number of positive or negative papers into an aggregate verdict. First establish that their constructs, interventions, outcomes and time horizons are comparable. These reviews strengthen that methodological rationale; they do not independently prove our conclusions.

### Autonomous Science Now Has a Useful Critique-and-Reanalysis Chain

[Into the Unknown](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19159) presents expert-specified follow-up problems and reports that no evaluated submission satisfies every criterion. Rubrics originate with scientists, but automated judges implement the checks. It is not a census of all AI-supported science. [@kimunknown2026]

[Alfaqeeh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38691), submitted 28 October, reanalyze that released benchmark and explicitly discuss InnovationEval. Their key distinction is between a number absent from artifacts and a real number assigned to the wrong experiment, metric or run. They also test sensitivity to another judge. This is additional analysis of shared outputs, not independent new agent trials. The checker establishes provenance, not scientific correctness; non-certification is not falsehood. [@alfaqeehprovenance2026]

[Luo, Kasirzadeh and Shah](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42832657/) provide a recently published journal account of workflow pitfalls. Its related preprint predates the journal publication by a year. Only the final abstract and bibliographic/conflict record were inspected here; do not claim a complete final-methods review or count both versions as independent studies. [@luopitfalls2026]

[Fujimura](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39620), submitted 29 October, supplies a constructive counterpart: consecutive agent-selected research runs with recorded re-computation and critique. The limitations disclose post-result amendments, only five runs, no ablation and AI-only external evaluation. Workshop-like ratings are not workshop acceptances. Hosted model computation is not a scientific process performed entirely on a laptop. [@fujimuraquestions2026]

**Synthesis implication:** require links from claims to experiments, not just the existence of a report or an executable script. Useful process innovations and unresolved quality limits can coexist. Misreporting cannot automatically be interpreted as intent to deceive.

The [new scientific-agent primer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43586-026-00534-y) helps organize workflows and evaluation questions. Its abstract and metadata resolve a previous discovery candidate, but do not constitute full-primer appraisal. [@fengprimer2026]

### Europe Adds Different Denominators, Not One More Productivity Rate

[Philip Lane's 30 October ECB keynote](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/key/date/2026/html/ecb.sp261030~08d9d540f0.en.html) explicitly cites the October revision of Firm Data on AI and brings firm and worker surveys together. The notes distinguish use intensity, perceived savings, conditional investment plans and a hypothetical productivity shock. [@laneadoption2026]

**Synthesis implication:** keep observation dates and populations beside adoption figures. Do not interpret survey-estimated savings as time logs or a macroeconomic scenario as realized growth. Reported task savings may capture respondents' total savings, not additive task increments.

### Mathematical Training Is Also an Institutional Investment

[Gireeja Ranade's 29 October guest essay](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/29/ai-mathematics-education-and-a-public-goods-problem/) on Tao's blog connects intellectual participation, training and early-career incentives. Ranade, not Tao, is the author. This is expert argument, not measured employment displacement. [@ranadepublic2026]

**Synthesis implication:** ask who finances practice and expertise when useful junior work can be delegated. We need not adopt the essay's categorical claim about AI augmentation being unable to generate human understanding; whether particular learning designs improve understanding remains empirically testable. Its institutional concern is separable from that claim.

## What Was Not Admitted

Recent summaries of InnovationEval, METR and the PostgreSQL account were useful discovery routes but did not establish independent replications. Some secondary reports also repeat older versions of sample sizes or effect estimates. Primary source/version checks take precedence over publication recency.

Curated or automated evidence hubs can supply leads, but their ratings and coverage were not validated. The log records these separately from admitted studies. Further priorities include the After42 expert-question evaluation, an additional practitioner review, the ECB-linked financial-inference paper, and complete final versions of partially accessible sources.

No exhaustive claim about the absence of follow-ups is justified: papers released days ago may not yet have indexed citations. No original third-party full text is redistributed, and this session is not a replacement for release-time verification.
