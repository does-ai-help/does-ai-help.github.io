# November Evidence Update: Capability, Work Design and Realized Benefit

Research session: **28 October 2026**. Status: **unpublished November preparation**. Sixteen newly appraised references join the 46 already staged. None is added to October's published evidence or artifacts.

This is a targeted update, not an exhaustive systematic review or meta-analysis. Selection favors contributions that change an interpretation, add a missing outcome or expose a measurement problem. Publication in 2026 does not mean the underlying observations concern 2026 tools. Identity verification, reading depth and independent validation are separate properties; the database records them separately.

## What Should Change in the Synthesis?

The strongest editorial implication is to distinguish **capability expansion, accepted output, retained understanding, organizational value and worker welfare**. A source can support one while saying little about the others. The additions below are not votes for or against AI and cannot be averaged into a single benefit percentage.

### 1. Helpful Does Not Necessarily Mean Easier

[Kewenig et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30945) test metacognitive scaffolding rather than AI access alone. Writing improves while perceived helpfulness falls. Crucially, the later task removes the scaffold, **not AI**; short-term topic recall also has substantial missingness. [@kewenigscaffolds2026]

**Editorial implication:** distinguish a pleasant interface from an effective intervention. Neither user enthusiasm nor discomfort alone establishes benefit or harm. Retention, unaided transfer and the cost of effort require separate tests.

[Bottesch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25922) find efficiency gains but task-dependent quality, including deterioration on knowledge acquisition. Their experiment used an early-2025 enterprise implementation. [@botteschfit2026]

**Editorial implication:** present speed and correctness side by side. A task-specific adverse result deserves prominence without becoming a timeless verdict on all later models.

### 2. More Possibilities and More Work Can Coexist

[Choudhuri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23708) add developer accounts of previously inaccessible and genuinely different work, not just faster execution. This extends the option-value argument already staged from Dan Luu. It is a self-report survey, not measured additional value. [@choudhuriunlock2026; @luuambitious2026]

[Laura Counts's Berkeley interview](https://vcresearch.berkeley.edu/news/does-ai-actually-free-workers-time) presents the other side: task expansion, boundary erosion and multitasking in Ye and Ranganathan's ethnography. The company had 200 employees; the study did **not** interview all 200. [@countsintensification2026]

**Editorial implication:** ask who chooses the additional work and who captures the gains. More achievable projects can be a genuine benefit while higher expectations absorb workers' saved time. Organizational output and personal welfare are not interchangeable endpoints.

### 3. Agentic Work Redistributes Human Attention

[Chou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33588) observe developers managing agents and checking responses. Their small shadowing sample is descriptive: management time is neither automatically overhead to eliminate nor proof of a slowdown. [@choumanagers2026]

[Ferino et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34317) distinguish several delegation choices and associate them with trust. Author-reported acceptance at ICSE 2027 is recorded, without inventing a published proceedings citation. [@ferinodelegation2026]

**Editorial implication:** measure review, coordination and learning alongside artifact production. Deliberately declining delegation can be rational use of judgment, not merely deficient adoption. Conversely, trusted output is not necessarily correct output.

### 4. Human Collaboration Does Not Simply Disappear

[Soda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32461) use a staged organizational rollout to estimate changes in collaboration and networks. Their results challenge a simple substitution story, but communication volume is not itself productive collaboration. [@sodanetworks2026]

**Editorial implication:** treat organizational relationships as an outcome, not a background assumption. More interpersonal checking can represent useful collective judgment, avoidable friction or both. A quasi-experiment still requires scrutiny of its identification assumptions.

### 5. Context Matters for Favorable Results Too

[Mockus et al.](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESEM.2026.93) trace AI completions into landed code at Meta. Their context analysis qualifies favorable review-time and incident associations. The study concerns **completion-era assistance**, not a universal test of autonomous agents. [@mockuscontext2026]

**Editorial implication:** place this alongside, not above, adverse telemetry. Task selection, codebase, tool generation and outcome definition can explain disagreement. Apply the same skepticism to positive and negative observational associations; controlling variables does not automatically identify causality.

### 6. Adoption Is Not Exposure, and Forecasts Are Not Outcomes

[Firm Data on AI](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34836) has an important **October revision**, expanding to nearly 9,000 executives in seven countries. It reports modest retrospective productivity effects and little net employment change, with offsetting changes across firms. These are executive assessments, not audited causal estimates. Earlier four-country figures must not be substituted for this version. [@yotzovfirm2026]

[Bick et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35677) distinguish reported task adoption from occupational exposure and provider chat logs. Broad availability can coexist with shallow, uneven use. Only public abstracts and metadata were inspected for these two working papers. [@bicktasks2026]

**Editorial implication:** report whose perspective each measure captures. A worker's actual use, an executive's retrospective attribution and an exposure model answer different questions. An aggregate employment balance can conceal substantial reallocation.

### 7. Geography Changes the Relevant Question

[Gmyrek, Viollaz and Winkler](https://www.ilo.org/publications/disruption-without-dividend-how-digital-divide-and-task-differences-split) analyze task differences and digital access across 135 countries. Their ILO/World Bank study describes potential exposure and constrained augmentation, not observed displacement. [@gmyrekdivide2026]

**Editorial implication:** avoid treating well-connected US technology firms as the default world. Access, institutions and local task composition may determine who can capture a technological opportunity. The relevant comparator may be unavailable expertise rather than a frontier professional workflow.

### 8. Science Includes Reuse and Verification, Not Only Discovery

[Paper2Agent](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11044-y) structures executable research resources and improves performance on tutorial-derived tasks. Its coverage failures and benchmark construction matter: executing a published workflow is not validating its scientific conclusions. [@miaopaper2agent2026]

[Coakley et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44488-026-00015-x) use AI to analyze documentation across a large historical corpus. Their estimated reproducibility is a **proxy**, not tens of thousands of successful replications. [@coakleyrepro2026]

**Editorial implication:** create a distinct scientific-workflow category between clerical assistance and autonomous discovery. Reducing the cost of inspecting, reusing or challenging prior work can be valuable even without a new theorem or laboratory discovery. The comparison must still include failure cases and validation work.

### 9. Diversity Depends Partly on Incentives

[Jo and Raghavan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03529) find that originality incentives change co-creation strategies and collective diversity in a preregistered writing experiment. This is a qualified counterpoint to homogenization claims, not evidence that incentives solve scientific diversity loss. [@joincentives2026]

**Editorial implication:** examine reward systems as well as model properties. Individual quality, collective diversity and novelty are different objectives and can require different interventions.

### 10. Resource Efficiency Needs an Honest Denominator

[Oviedo et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/energy-use-of-ai-inference-efficiency-pathways-and-test-time-scaling/) model inference under deployment assumptions; [David Mytton](https://www.devsustainability.com/p/energy-use-of-ai-inference-estimates) explains accounting boundaries and hidden agent calls. They are **one study family plus commentary**, not two empirical replications. [@oviedoenergy2026; @myttonenergy2026]

**Editorial implication:** compare resources per useful, accepted outcome, including repeated attempts and checking, rather than one visible prompt. Neither a small modeled short-query cost nor large aggregate data-center demand alone answers whether a particular use is worthwhile.

## Proposed November Article Passage

Recent evidence makes the question of help more precise rather than resolving it universally. Assistance may improve a particular output without reducing effort, enlarge the range of feasible work without freeing workers' time, and lower the cost of reusing scientific methods without validating the science itself. At the organizational level, adoption statistics, perceived gains and realized outcomes remain distinct. Benefits and costs can also fall on different people.

The appropriate unit of evaluation is therefore the complete human and institutional workflow: what becomes possible, what is accepted as correct, what people retain, which resources are consumed, and who benefits. Positive findings should not erase adverse outcomes; adverse findings should not erase valuable new capabilities. Neither should be generalized beyond the intervention, population, period and outcome actually studied.
