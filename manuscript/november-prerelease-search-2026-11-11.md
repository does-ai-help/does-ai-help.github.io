# Pre-release Search: Evidence Beyond Generation Speed

Search and selected inspections: **11 November 2026**. Planned publication target: **13 November**, not an evidence cutoff or completed release. Ten further sources are admitted after deduplication. Together with today's reader-suggested batches, the draft now contains **358 references**, including **129 November additions**. These are not counts of independent experiments. [Search log](../november/search-2026-11-11-prerelease.json).

## Search Approach

Searches covered recent software-engineering and agent evaluations, knowledge-worker learning, organizational outcomes, scientific feedback and evaluation, physical research workflows, well-being and adoption. Publisher, arXiv, NBER and institutional pages supplied discovery routes. Selection favored primary evidence and methodological relevance, not novelty of a headline alone.

Searches constrained to November arXiv identifiers did not reliably return fresh material. Older but previously uncollected studies were therefore considered, with original dates retained. This is a targeted narrative-review update, not a reproducible census of all eligible publications. It does not establish that no important work was missed.

## What the Admitted Sources Add

### Assisted Capability Does Not Automatically Become Expertise

[Wiles and colleagues' final article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02566-6), published 26 October, is directly relevant to task expansion. The final abstract supports a favorable performance result and a null unaided-knowledge result, not either universal deskilling or universal upskilling. An earlier author copy has a different participant count and is not substituted for final methods. [@wilestemporary2026]

[(Im)Paired Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26375) complements this with code comprehension rather than general technical knowledge. Its follow-up extension results require care: initial task success and subsequent understanding pull in different directions. Do not simplify the paper to a claim that every later outcome deteriorated. The study compares two AI interaction modes, not AI with no AI. [@balepurunderstanding2026]

These additions strengthen the rationale for measuring assisted task performance, understanding of the resulting artifact, immediate transfer and durable skill separately. Their findings do not make those outcomes interchangeable or establish permanent effects.

### The Next Useful Denominator Can Be Organizational

[Barwick and colleagues' November working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w36003) directly examines firm sales following platform-embedded AI adoption. Its identification and large reported effects deserve full-text scrutiny before receiving the weight of a completed methodological appraisal. For now the primary indexed abstract supports a narrow description, not endorsement of a general growth estimate. [@barwickshared2026]

The [revised voice-interview experiment](https://luca-henkel.github.io/papers/InterviewAI.pdf) offers a different organizational design: AI collects information while humans decide. Absolute and relative offer effects differ substantially. The authors disclose research support and a later unpaid role at the partner firm. It is stronger evidence than executive testimony about recruiting, but does not settle fairness or generalize automatically to expert hiring. [@jabarianvoice2025]

[Hu and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30725) add a concrete engineering-cost mechanism. Benchmark inference savings are not total cost savings; apparently helpful retrieval tooling can add overhead. This supports measuring interventions rather than assuming that more agent machinery pays for itself. [@hucostskills2026]

### Scientific Assistance Has Different Roles and Risks

[AutoDNA](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11228-6), published 4 November, is a useful physical-workflow counterpart to text-based agent demonstrations. It concerns a co-designed laboratory, not generic scientific autonomy. The public abstract is insufficient for a full appraisal of methods, failure rates or total costs. [@wuautodna2026]

[Wang and colleagues' feedback experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24180) supplies causal evidence on researcher behavior, not proof that the resulting science improved. Its small absolute revision increment should accompany the relative description, and detector-based longer-run measures need separate caution. The intervention occurred in 2024, despite the 2026 paper date. [@wangfeedback2026]

[Machado's grant-selection study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-026-05792-x) is a relevant adverse result about evaluation. Human-rated novelty is associated with disagreement, but the comparison does not establish novelty as the causal mechanism. The model's missing explicit rubric is an important asymmetry. Retain this concern without treating human decisions as an infallible standard or next-token prediction as a demonstrated explanation. [@machadonovelty2026]

The contrast matters: feedback that prompts revision, selection of projects and operation of laboratory instruments are different tasks. A beneficial result in one cannot resolve risks in another.

### Perceived Value and Well-being Need Their Own Measures

The [Ghanaian adult-learning study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-78289-5), published 6 November, adds a setting underrepresented in the engineering literature. Qualitative selection deliberately foregrounds difficulties. Those narratives are useful for mechanisms and lived experience, not their population frequency or causal clinical effects. [@adarkwahwellbeing2026]

[Gallup's synthesis, updated 10 November](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/713063/ai-workplace-productivity.aspx), adds current presentation of perceived benefits. An updated web page is not a new survey. Associations with managerial support or breadth of use should not become prescriptions to maximize adoption, particularly when telemetry and accepted outcomes may differ. [@gallupproductivity2026]

## Selection, Access and Remaining Gaps

Already-collected productivity meta-analysis and Hao's science-focus study were not duplicated. Secondary reports, evidence hubs and journal calls supplied leads but were not admitted merely for appearing recent. A community simulation was not used as observed developer behavior; generated scientific results were not promoted through an expert blog citation.

New full-text priorities are the final Wiles article, AutoDNA and NBER 36003. Public abstracts are clearly labeled and support only narrow statements. Other selected studies received methods-level reading, but no underlying datasets, experiments or proof artifacts were reproduced. All inspections have dated provenance; no release-wide audit or new Chrome verification is claimed.

For the 13 November target, the remaining work is editorial and evidentiary as well as technical: resolve high-impact version/access questions where feasible, refresh designated browser checks and reference identities, review numerical claims, then approve and tag the final release. The current preview must remain explicitly provisional until those steps are complete.
