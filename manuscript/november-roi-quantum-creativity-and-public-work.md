## 1. A Bad Headline Is Not a New Base Rate

[Coffee Cup AI's commentary](https://coffeecupai.com/blog/95-ai-failure-stat-one-year-later-junk/) challenges the familiar failure-rate claim and argues that organizational obstacles remain important. We retain it as an attributed practitioner critique, not an independently verified reconstruction of NANDA's data. The original PDF and linked Wiblin critique were inaccessible in this inspection. [@coffeefailure2026]

The [primary McKinsey survey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai) is separately admitted. It supports a distinction between perceived individual gains and reported enterprise financial impact. Respondents also report nonfinancial and function-level benefits. [@mckinseyroi2026]

**Our assessment:**

- No enterprise EBIT attribution is not equivalent to no useful outcome, no ROI, or a failed pilot. These have different denominators, thresholds and observation periods.
- Associations between redesign and reported success do not establish that redesign alone causes success. The commentary's conclusion that models were never the bottleneck is too categorical for this evidence.
- Comparing executive samples with business-register surveys requires matched populations, firm sizes, dates and definitions. Their difference is not automatically an estimate of executive misperception.
- Low-intensity or informal use does not establish unauthorized use. A claim about shadow AI needs direct evidence about permission and governance.
- Neither the disputed headline nor another survey's proportion should become a universal success probability for budget decisions.

The appropriate November addition is a short account of **measurement and citation drift**, linked to the existing discussion of adoption, verification and value. Do not reproduce the blog's entire numerical ledger as independently checked evidence.

## 2. Quantum Mathematics: Scope Before Spectacle

[Joseph Howlett's report](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-just-scooped-human-researchers-on-a-million-dollar-quantum-math-problem/) adds interviews and a contested release chronology. The current publisher heading differs from the URL title; both are recorded. The main article was accessible in local Chrome. [@howlettquantum2026]

The existing [OpenAI announcement](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/) describes an existence claim. [Clay's official problem](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf) requires a suitable four-dimensional theory **and a positive mass gap**. Do not present the narrower claim as the complete Millennium solution. [@openaiunderstanding2026]

**Our assessment:**

- Separate an announcement, a public proof, a checked formal statement and independent mathematical acceptance. The same word, verification, should not hide these different stages.
- Statements about discovery dates, overlap, scooping or formalization duration remain attributed. This intake does not adjudicate priority or allege appropriation.
- Reported progress by competing researchers also used AI. This is not a clean comparison of unaided humans against a model.
- The counterpoint to capability expansion is institutional: access, attribution, release timing and the distribution of checking work affect whether a result becomes shared knowledge.

This belongs alongside Calegari, Strogatz and the scientific-stewardship material, not in a table of independently validated discoveries. [@calegarichanged2026; @strogatzfuture2026]

## 3. Creativity: An Artifact Does Not Document Its Maker's Learning

[The Brighter Side of News explainer](https://www.thebrighterside.news/post/the-age-of-ai-is-forcing-humanity-to-rethink-what-it-means-to-be-creative/) leads to [Amit Raphael Zoran's paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-026-03379-x), DOI **10.1007/s00146-026-03379-x**. We record Yarden Mills as the explainer's credited writer and Shy Cohen separately as editor. The two records concern one conceptual contribution, not two studies. [@millscreativity2026; @zoranbecoming2026]

The paper distinguishes creative output from developmental experience. It explicitly does not measure transformation in its illustrative interventions.

**Our assessment:** this provides a useful conceptual bridge to assisted performance versus learning. It does not prove deskilling, reduced human creativity or the disappearance of meaningful AI-supported practice. The normative emphasis on embodied experience is a position to discuss, not a universal empirical criterion. An alternative learning pathway can still be demanding and valuable; recipient and maker outcomes also need not move together.

## 4. Dutch Civil-Service Reductions: Intentions Are Not Displacement Estimates

[Bloomberg's report](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/dutch-government-plans-to-cut-25-000-jobs-in-ai-led-overhaul) is retained with its restricted reading scope. Title, author, date and public opening were inspected, not the complete subscriber article. The substantive policy account below rests on the [official government statement](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/10/26/kabinet-werkt-aan-slagvaardige-overheid-eenvoudiger-slimmer-en-kleiner). [@vanoosteromcuts2026; @dutchrestructuring2026]

The government estimates a multi-year reduction of approximately **25,000 FTE**, alongside savings targets extending to 2035. AI-specific targets and savings plans are due in the first quarter of 2027. Non-AI measures include changing tasks, rules, ICT and property arrangements.

**Our assessment:** this is evidence that anticipated AI capabilities can enter workforce planning, not evidence that the projected technological substitution has happened. Distinguish posts, FTE, headcount, vacancies, attrition and dismissals. Do not attribute the whole package to AI, assume service quality is preserved, or equate projected fiscal savings with a measured net social benefit.

## Proposed Article Passage

Public debate often compresses a sequence of distinct claims. A project without reported financial impact becomes a failed project; a narrower research announcement becomes a solved grand challenge; sophisticated output becomes evidence of human learning; and a planned staffing reduction becomes realized technological displacement. Each transition requires evidence that the preceding claim alone does not supply. The November update should therefore track both the source and the status of a claim: what was proposed, observed, independently checked and ultimately used.
