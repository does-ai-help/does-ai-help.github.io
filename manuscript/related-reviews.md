# Companion reviews: October 2026 search update

Search conducted on 13 October 2026, Europe/Warsaw. Sources available by the existing 12 October evidence cutoff are eligible. This targeted search is not exhaustive or PRISMA-compliant. Eleven new sources were added and two existing entries received abstract-level appraisal. Secondary syntheses are not additional independent experiments.

## Search approach

Queried systematic reviews, meta-analyses, evidence syntheses and technical surveys on knowledge-work productivity, software engineering, learning, scientific discovery and human-AI collaboration. Followed results to arXiv, publisher sites, conference material, OECD, ILO and Stanford HAI. Matched identifiers against the database and separated revisions from new works. Search snippets served discovery, not result verification. Inspected scope is recorded in `data/related-reviews.json`; metadata are in `data/audit-related.json`.

## Interpretation

The new sources strengthen outcome distinctions and show why learning should not receive a uniformly negative verdict. Education syntheses disagree: populations, outcomes, eligibility rules and statistical models differ. Resolve these differences by examining included trials, not voting among headlines. Shared trials are not independent corroboration. No cross-review pooled estimate is calculated here.

A 2026 review may primarily represent 2022-2024 interventions. We retain evidence windows and version dates separately, and do not count preprints and journal versions twice. The scientific-agent survey maps capabilities; it does not establish reliable general autonomy. CoSLR was retained for process relevance, not as certification of this publication.

## Screening limits

The search also surfaced single-author quantitative reviews, conceptual frameworks and narrower medical or project-estimation reviews. These were not admitted solely on title or snippet evidence. Selection prioritized relevance, inspectable scope, primary identity and coverage gaps. Non-inclusion is not a judgment of invalidity. Future work should reconcile educational meta-analyses at trial level and appraise additional narrower syntheses.
