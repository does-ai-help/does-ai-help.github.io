## Interpretation for the Review

The following are our editorial questions rather than findings established by the interview:

- **Access and understanding are different goods.** Making an artifact available can facilitate scrutiny, but does not automatically make it usable. Conversely, delaying release to improve legibility can restrict participation. Compare explicit release arrangements rather than treating all withholding or all immediate disclosure alike.
- **A public-interest rationale needs procedural scrutiny.** We should represent the case for accelerating access fairly, while asking who selects reviewers, which materials become public and who bears the verification cost. These questions connect the existing stewardship and expert-perspective sources. [@openaiunderstanding2026; @howartheth2026; @howlettquantum2026]
- **A spending claim is not a cost-effectiveness result.** Before comparing AI with human research, require a stated boundary: training, inference, unsuccessful searches, verification, exposition and infrastructure may be counted differently. Distinguish a proof-generation budget from the cost of producing accepted, reusable knowledge.
- **Unreleased discoveries are not established capability outcomes.** They may motivate follow-up, but should not enter a count of validated successes or imply a rate of future discovery.
- **The controversy is not simply progress versus resistance.** Calegari's proposals, researchers' concerns and the developer's argument can all recognize potential capability while disagreeing about how its value should be realized. [@calegarichanged2026]

The dispute also concerns how the value of discovery is made public. Reporting on Altman's defense of continued mathematical releases adds an explicit developer rationale, but does not resolve questions of correctness, access or verification burden.
