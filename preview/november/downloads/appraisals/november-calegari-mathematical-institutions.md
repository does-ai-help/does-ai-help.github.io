## Contribution

Calegari connects expanded research possibilities with institutional incentives. He proposes rewarding understanding, question selection and exposition, changing hiring and journals, and broadening education without abandoning technical competence. He also criticizes attribution and reliability problems. These are proposals and judgments, not evaluated interventions.

## How It Fits the Review

The following are our editorial interpretations, not additional empirical findings from the essay:

- **Extend the option-value argument.** Our section on previously infeasible work should include the institutions that decide whether such exploration is funded and credited. Lower generation costs need not remove the bottleneck in choosing worthwhile research. [@luuambitious2026; @calegarichanged2026]
- **Connect capability with participation.** Read alongside Strogatz and Math 2.0, the essay shifts attention from the existence of proofs to the human and organizational practices surrounding them. Preserve distinct positions rather than presenting a unified mathematical-community response. [@strogatzfuture2026; @taomath2026]
- **Make evaluation an explicit outcome.** A useful November question is whether AI changes what an institution rewards, not merely how quickly its members produce work. This can inform comparisons with education and engineering without assuming reforms transfer unchanged across professions.

## Critical Boundaries

The essay does not independently certify the correctness, novelty or scope of announced proofs. Those claims still require artifact-specific checking and specialist assessment. Keep it separate from the OpenAI announcement and existing community statements. [@openaiunderstanding2026; @mathmisalignment2026; @leiden2026]

Our further concerns about the proposed reforms are:

- Oral interviews may expose gaps in understanding, but can also amplify communication, disability, language and prestige biases. Their fairness and predictive validity require evidence.
- AI-assisted evaluation standards need safeguards for unequal tool access and changing model capability. They should not be interpreted as permission to abandon disclosure.
- Collective acknowledgment of mathematics' foundations cannot replace specific contributor credit, provenance or accountability.
- Breadth and technical depth can compete for training time. A defensible curriculum needs assessed learning outcomes, not simply fewer technical exercises.

These concerns qualify the proposal; they do not establish that the existing evaluation system is preferable.

## Proposed Article Addition

Institutional adaptation is a further dimension of assistance. In a guest essay on Terence Tao's blog, Frank Calegari argues for changing mathematical evaluation, publication and training to emphasize understanding and research direction. This is a reform agenda, not evidence that particular reforms work. Its implication for this review is to ask whether institutions reward consequential intellectual contributions or simply the artifacts whose production AI makes cheaper. [@calegarichanged2026]
