Its reported agreements concern **the company's capacity**, not the world's output. Shortage projections are guidance, not an established future outcome. [@skhynixsupply2026]

## What It Adds

The following are our analytical questions, not empirical findings established by the interview or release.

1. **Utilization is not the same as useful value.** Pooling may be an efficiency mechanism, but a comparison needs the complete workload and accepted outcome, not just time spent active. A hardware-utilization claim alone cannot settle net benefit.
2. **Choose the counterfactual explicitly.** Using existing personal capacity, buying a new local system, and procuring new data-center capacity are different comparisons. Include equipment lifetime, network dependence, repeated attempts and verification work where relevant.
3. **Scarcity has distributional consequences.** Ask who obtains the resource, who pays for it and who loses access to alternatives. Do not infer a measured consumer-price effect from a supplier's optimistic outlook alone.
4. **Control also has value.** Privacy, offline availability, latency requirements, data governance and independence from a provider can matter even if centralized infrastructure achieves higher utilization. Their importance varies by use case; this is not a blanket argument for local inference.
5. **Keep technical categories and denominators separate.** Consumer memory, server products, HBM and NAND should not be treated as one interchangeable supply pool. Company-specific contracts do not establish market-wide allocation shares.

Altman leads a cloud AI provider, and SK hynix benefits from memory demand. Those interests do not refute their claims, but make independent workload comparisons and supply evidence important. The article's quoted low local-utilization figure should not become a general parameter in our analysis without supporting measurements.

## Integration Decision

Include a short **resource allocation and agency** example in the cost/sustainability discussion. Pair it with the existing energy-accounting material, not with experimental productivity estimates. Environmental efficiency, financial cost and user autonomy are separate outcomes; none should silently stand in for the others. [@oviedoenergy2026; @myttonenergy2026]

### Proposed Article Passage

Infrastructure efficiency can also become a dispute about who controls computing resources. Reporting on Altman's preference for cloud memory allocation illustrates the tension, rather than resolving it. Evaluating assistance requires attention to utilization and useful output, but also to access, privacy and the ability to work independently of a provider. Supplier forecasts offer context for scarcity; they are not proof that any one allocation maximizes social value. [@bonifieldram2026; @skhynixsupply2026]
