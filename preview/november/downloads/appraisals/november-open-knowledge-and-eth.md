## Open Knowledge Is Both an Input and a Community

[Lauren Feiner's article](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1022880/wikipedia-openai-new-york-times-copyright) reports Wikimedia's objection to treating all AI uses alike. Chrome exposed a public preview, followed by a subscription restriction. The substantive appraisal relies on the [Foundation's public statement](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/10/30/wikimedia-foundation-amicus-brief-new-york-times-v-open-ai-case/) and [original proposed filing](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:26.10.29_WMF_motion_and_amicus_In_re_OAI.pdf). [@feinerwikimedia2026; @wmfknowledge2026; @wmfbrief2026]

Wikimedia favors differentiated treatment of training and source-substituting retrieval. The filing also describes benefits to editors, alongside concerns about the knowledge ecosystem. Its retrieval discussion is not entirely categorical: footnote 24 recognizes licensed sources and fact-dependent exceptions.

**What this adds to our review:** the relevant unit of benefit may be an ecosystem, not just an individual answer. A service can make information easier to obtain while changing the incentives or resources available to its producers. Conversely, protecting producers in one way could restrict useful tools. This is a substantive institutional tradeoff, not a reason to assume either harm or net benefit without measurement.

**Boundaries:**

- These are arguments to a court, not newly settled law. A motion seeking leave and its proposed attachment do not establish acceptance or judicial endorsement.
- The document's filing date is **29 October**; the announcement and Commons upload are dated **30 October**. They are one episode, not independent confirmations of impact.
- Do not flatten the position into either unrestricted AI reuse or a blanket ban on retrieval. Do not treat any attribution link as automatic compliance with all relevant obligations.
- Reported editorial assistance is not an LLM productivity experiment. Claims about traffic, costs and volunteer recruitment require appropriately designed evidence.

The editorial opportunity is to add **maintenance of shared knowledge** to the review's assessment of consequential value. We need evidence about continuing source production and participation, as well as access to existing material.

## ETH: A Claim About Complexity Is Not a Measured Engineering Gain

[Tom Howarth's ScienceInsider report](https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-cracks-27-year-old-pillar-computer-science-openai-and-anthropic-claim), DOI **10.1126/science.z3zo39t**, was published on **28 October**. It reports competing ETH claims, limited immediate practical implications and differing disclosure arrangements. It is journalism, not a peer-reviewed mathematical result. [@howartheth2026]

The underlying OpenAI announcement and a pinned artifact-scope observation already exist in the November intake. The report adds expert interviews and reception; it does not convert that existing record into an independently validated discovery. [@openaiunderstanding2026]

**Our interpretation:**

- Keep proof-checker acceptance, fidelity to the intended statement, human understanding and community acceptance distinct. This appraisal checks none of the proofs independently.
- A claimed ETH refutation is not a resolution of P versus NP, a demonstrated practical solver improvement or a cryptographic break.
- Two companies' accounts are not automatically two independent replications. Provenance, shared prior work, precise theorem statements and artifacts need examination.
- The choice of initial reviewers affects access and perceived legitimacy. Expertise can be valuable without company-appointed stewardship becoming the only acceptable model.
- Interviewees' assessments reflect a dated report. Do not present its uncertainty or its enthusiasm as the community's settled position on 1 November.

This material belongs next to the existing discussion of scientific stewardship, Yang-Mills reporting and Calegari's institutional proposals, rather than in the experimental-productivity table. [@howlettquantum2026; @calegarichanged2026]

## Proposed Article Addition

AI assistance depends on institutions that produce and maintain reliable knowledge. Wikimedia's proposed intervention in AI copyright litigation illustrates that useful tools and a sustainable source ecosystem need not be aligned automatically. Separately, reporting on the ETH claims illustrates a further gap between producing a formal artifact and developing shared scientific understanding. These cases do not supply comparable effect sizes. They motivate a broader evaluation: does assistance preserve the conditions for future verification, participation and knowledge creation? [@wmfbrief2026; @howartheth2026]
