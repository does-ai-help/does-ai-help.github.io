## What the Case Adds

Recruiting connects administrative efficiency, consequential selection and learning. These outcomes should be separated. The following are our appraisal questions, not additional findings about Accenture's system.

### Process Speed Versus Decision Quality

Administrative automation could leave more time for conversations and sourcing. But shorter recruitment time does not establish better hires, fairer access or lower total cost. A candidate-quality improvement needs a definition, baseline, validation period and denominator before it can be compared with other studies.

For a stronger evaluation, request role and cohort composition, the comparison process, attrition and retention, later job performance, applicant experience, false exclusions, recruiter effort and system costs. Report these separately rather than compressing them into one quality score.

### A Human Final Decision Is Not an End-to-End Audit

The company's stated human role is relevant, but it does not show what happens before a candidate reaches the hiring manager. Meaningful oversight requires visibility into screening, ranking and exclusions as well as final decisions. This is a general evaluation principle, not a finding that Accenture discriminated or denied review.

### Billable Productivity Versus Durable Skill

The onboarding claim is relevant to entry-level pathways, but productive deployment, learning and independent competence are different endpoints. Neither deskilling nor lasting skill improvement follows automatically. Assessment should include retention, transfer, supervision burden and performance when assistance is unavailable, alongside time to useful work.

## How to Use the Historical Critique

The 2022 paper analyzes recruitment vendors' marketing and assumptions about neutrality. It is not a contemporary agent benchmark. Its useful question is whether removing explicit demographic attributes removes the organizational and social processes affecting assessment. It does not establish the performance or fairness of Accenture's current tools, nor justify labeling all recruiting automation pseudoscience.

The chronology matters: older criticisms identify questions to investigate, while new deployment claims require new evidence. Neither novelty nor age settles validity.

## Integration Decision

Include as an **attributed organizational case**, not a causal field experiment. Its positive claims broaden the review beyond coding and make onboarding a concrete topic. The limits are substantive: administrative efficiency, selection validity, applicant welfare and durable learning require distinct measurements.

### Proposed Article Passage

Recruitment illustrates why a complete workflow evaluation matters. Accenture reports faster hiring and onboarding from agent-assisted processes, with final hiring decisions remaining human. Those statements identify a plausible use and a company-defined outcome, but leave open the definitions, comparison groups and effects on applicants. A review should assess useful administrative assistance without treating process speed as proof of selection fairness or faster entry into billable work as proof of durable learning.
