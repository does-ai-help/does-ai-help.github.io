## Proposed Article Passage

AI-enabled discovery can increase downstream remediation demand rather than immediately reduce labor. CNBC reports Bank of America's CEO describing 1,000 contractors added in Q2 alongside 3,000 existing staff for patching. The reported Q3 expense combines software remediation with anti-money-laundering compliance, so it cannot be assigned to Mythos alone. The bank's forecast savings from internal AI projects do not establish a realized net return. This is a company account, not a causal employment estimate or an independent security audit. [@sonpatching2026]

## Interpretation and Limits

The useful mechanism is **discovery versus resolution**. Finding a vulnerability, establishing that it matters, preparing a patch, testing it and safely deploying it are different tasks. Better detection can expose an existing backlog and create valuable work. It can also increase defensive costs if attackers gain capability. The present report does not quantify either avoided losses or the incremental threat. These are competing mechanisms to investigate, not net effects established by this case.

Do not describe newly exposed vulnerabilities as necessarily introduced by AI-generated code. Nor should more security spending automatically be labeled waste: without measuring risk reduction and the no-AI counterfactual, its welfare effect remains uncertain. Conversely, an executive's expectation that costs will decline is not an observed decline.

Contractors, employees and net employment are different measures. External hiring in one activity can coexist with employee reductions elsewhere. Do not offset the contractor count against the report's bank-wide headcount change or present either as an identified economy-wide employment effect. Link this case conceptually to the Microsoft workforce announcement and Reuters contracting report, without treating them as matched populations or independent causal studies. [@stewartbuyouts2026; @reutersitcontracts2026]

For a subsequent appraisal, seek vulnerability counts and severity, independently validated fixes, remediation throughput, cost allocation, contractor duration and realized savings. Different periods and mixed cost categories prevent a simple comparison of quarterly remediation expense with forecast annual savings. Do not manufacture a net-benefit calculation from those headline figures.
