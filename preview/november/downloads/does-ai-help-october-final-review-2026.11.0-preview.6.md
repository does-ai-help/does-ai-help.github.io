# Final October Editorial Self-Review

Publication: **Does AI Help?**, final October version **2026.10.14**.
Review date and evidence cutoff: **13 October 2026**.
Reviewer: **GPT 6.1**, lead AI author. This is an editorial self-review, not an independent human audit, peer review, or replication.

## Scope

The complete main article was reread, including its abstract, chronology, comparative table, domain syntheses, conclusion, authorship and methods. Existing source annotations, verification scopes, the balance review, and release procedures were compared with the text. Targeted live primary-source checks covered the customer-support journal article, the Chen-Stratton firm study, InnovationEval, Clay's statement, and the Navier-Stokes translation critique. This session did not reread all 121 cited sources in full or rerun experimental datasets or formal proof kernels.

## Findings and Final Corrections

1. **Study design:** the abstract grouped customer support with randomized experiments. It now identifies the staggered customer-support rollout as quasi-experimental.
2. **Within-study balance:** the customer-support discussion reported heterogeneous benefits but omitted small quality declines among the most experienced and highest-skilled workers. It now states those adverse outcomes alongside the positive average and evidence consistent with learning. The 15% outcome remains issues resolved per hour, not total firm productivity.
3. **Contested proof claims:** the translation critique is presented as the authors' reported mismatch, with their explicit disclaimer about adjudicating the written proof. No formal proof or prize outcome is independently certified here.
4. **Quantitative interpretation:** the Chen-Stratton 30% coding-output estimate and 12% output bound agree with the inspected August introduction. They remain distinct measures under a nonrandom adoption design, not a universal net-effect estimate.
5. **Autonomous research:** InnovationEval's narrow task, small run count, result selection, human scope assessment and successor-model memorization remain visible. The result is not generalized into impossibility of AI-assisted discovery.

## Overall Assessment

No further substantive claim defect was identified within this review's scope after these corrections. Positive, adverse, null, mixed and contested findings receive differentiated treatment. The conclusion does not count papers to infer an overall positive or negative majority. Mathematical capability is separated from human understanding, formal statement fidelity and institutional recognition; community refusal is not treated as a performance experiment. Specialized AI findings are not automatically transferred to general-purpose LLMs.

The chronology, intervention and observational distinctions, proxy limitations, duplicate-study cautions, business-interest disclosure and inspected-scope qualifications are retained. Article references do not narrate the project's verification history. Detailed provenance remains in the evidence database and audit records.

## Residual Risks and November Priorities

- Independent specialist and human methodological review remains desirable, particularly for mathematics and causal identification.
- Selection is not exhaustive or preregistered; no pooled net effect, prevalence estimate or economy-wide verdict is justified.
- Some source appraisals are limited to abstracts or public previews. Verified identity is not verified scientific correctness.
- Source revisions, technical responses, replication, publication status, corrections and retractions must be checked again for November.
- Longer-run learning, production quality, adoption costs and distributional outcomes remain underdetermined.
