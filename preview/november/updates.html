November 2026 Preview: Evidence Update

Version 2026.11.0-preview.6, prepared 12 November 2026. This is a working draft for the public preview, not a final monthly release. October version 2026.10.15 remains the published edition. The live preview advances when the preparation branch is pushed and deployed.

Scale and Scope

The preview combines 229 October records with 152 new records: 381 references. It also substantively appraises one existing reference, the CHI 2025 critical-thinking survey, without counting it twice. Records include primary studies, linked reporting, expert commentary, project notices and security advisories; they are not 381 independent studies.

The additional 1 November citation-following search admits eleven sources and records seventeen inspected citation links. Its main additions are task-expansion field evidence, cognition reviews, scientific-agent validation and reanalysis, European adoption synthesis and the institutional financing of expertise. Machine-readable search log.

The 11 November source appraisal adds twelve records: seven reader-suggested sources and five linked primary or methodological materials. It covers AlphaProof Code, contrasting engineering experiences, AI-assisted AI research, valuation arguments, PHP2Shell exploitability and ActivTrak telemetry. Selection and link-following log.

A second suggested-source appraisal adds nine records on CPython/Julia/PTX formalization, engineering judgment, selected agent demonstrations and Alpine/OpenSSH security. Follow-up log.

The pre-release cross-topic search admits ten more sources on assisted capability versus knowledge, scientific feedback and evaluation, physical laboratories, hiring, firm sales, agent costs and well-being. Search and selection log.

The 12 November assessment appraisal adds nine records, following reader-suggested reporting to the OfS/Advance HE report, detector research and its correction, product assessments and school policies. It distinguishes assessment validity from authorship classification and policy announcements from observed outcomes. Selection and link-following log. The intended publication target is 13 November; this preview's provisional evidence cutoff is 12 November, and final release checks remain pending.

The Asahi/Omarchy M case appraisal adds nine records on provenance, authorship, licensing, funding and reciprocity. It documents the prominent public interpretation while separating allegations from established repository facts, and funding commitments from received cash. Online search and source-selection log.

The Apache HTTP/2 appraisal adds five records and connects this critical finding to PHP2Shell, shared Brotli exposure and *sshole within the 21 October-11 November public-fix window. OpenSSH's move to more frequent releases supplies concrete evidence of a maintenance response. A possible new norm of compressed high-impact patch cycles is presented as a planning hypothesis, not a measured historical comparison. Selection log.

The update is a targeted narrative-review extension, not an exhaustive systematic search or a pooled effect estimate. Reading scopes vary and are recorded in the evidence map. Source families link overlapping reports and related advisories.

Changes to the Synthesis

The core question becomes more precise: does AI expand feasible work, improve accepted outcomes, preserve capability and produce value for the people bearing its costs? Those outcomes can diverge. Useful defect discovery can increase a maintenance backlog. Assistance can enlarge the project portfolio without freeing workers' time. Scientific reuse can improve without confirming the underlying science.

Positive findings, adverse results, null or modest organizational effects and explicit objections are retained under the same standards. Employer policies, court advocacy, philosophical arguments and technical studies have different evidentiary roles; none becomes a vote in a for-or-against tally.

Verification and Outstanding Work

  • Selected source content and identity checks are retained with their actual dates and reading scopes. No new release-wide live audit is claimed by this preview build.
  • Local Chrome observations and supplied-document provenance remain distinct. Historical October observations are not November release receipts.
  • Several NBER, ILO and Joule entries still require deeper full-text follow-up. Paywalled and clipped reading scopes remain explicit.
  • Mathematical claims are not independently certified here. Linux implementation status, project milestones and prospective release dates need current checks before final publication.
  • AlphaProof artifacts have not been rebuilt here; the full unredacted METR assessment and underlying ActivTrak telemetry are unavailable. These boundaries are not resolved by the existence of public summaries.
  • The final Wiles article, AutoDNA methods and NBER 36003 need full-text follow-up. An earlier Wiles author PDF has a different sample size and is not silently substituted for the final article.
  • Refresh selected browser checks, audit live reference identities and access, review source revisions, and complete editorial approval before a formal November release.
